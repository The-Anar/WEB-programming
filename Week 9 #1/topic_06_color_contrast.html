Color Contrast

Color contrast determines how easily users can distinguish text from its background. Good contrast helps people with low vision, color blindness, and other visual difficulties read and understand website content.

01 · Introduction

Why Color Contrast Matters

Text must be distinguishable from its background. If the contrast is too low, some users may struggle to read the content, even when the font is large.

Low contrast

Hard to read

Similar foreground and background colors make the text difficult to distinguish.

High contrast

Easy to read

A stronger difference between the foreground and background improves readability.

Remember: Do not rely on color alone to communicate important information. Text labels, icons, and other visual cues can help users understand content.
02 · WCAG contrast requirements

Understanding Contrast Ratios

A contrast ratio compares the relative luminance of foreground and background colors. The ratio ranges from 1:1 to 21:1.

4.5:1 AA — Normal Text

Minimum contrast ratio for normal-sized text.

3:1 AA — Large Text

Minimum contrast ratio for large text.

7:1 AAA — Text

Higher contrast requirement for enhanced accessibility.

21:1 Maximum Ratio

The maximum possible contrast, black against white.

Large text definition: At least 18pt regular text, or 14pt bold text. These sizes require a minimum 3:1 contrast ratio for WCAG AA.

06 · Summary

What We Learned

  • Color contrast helps users distinguish text from its background.
  • WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for normal-sized text.
  • WCAG AA requires 3:1 for large text.
  • WCAG AAA requires 7:1 for text.
  • Contrast ratios compare the relative luminance of two colors.
  • WebAIM Contrast Checker and Chrome DevTools can help evaluate color contrast.
Main takeaway: A beautiful design is not enough. Text must also be readable and accessible to everyone.

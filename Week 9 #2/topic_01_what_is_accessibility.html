What is Accessibility?

People use the web in different ways. Accessibility makes websites usable for everyone, including people with disabilities.

UNDERSTANDING DIFFERENT USERS

Four Types of Accessibility Needs

A website should not assume that everyone sees, hears, moves, or processes information in the same way.

👁️

01 · Visual Impairment

Some users have low vision or cannot see the screen. They may use screen readers such as NVDA to hear descriptions of webpage content.

Accessibility solution: Add meaningful alt text, sufficient color contrast, and keyboard-accessible navigation.

🖐️

02 · Motor Disability

Some users cannot easily use a mouse or touchpad. They may navigate using a keyboard, switch device, or other assistive technology.

Accessibility solution: Provide keyboard support, large interactive targets, and a logical focus order.

🧠

03 · Cognitive Differences

People with dyslexia, attention difficulties, or other cognitive differences may find complicated layouts and long instructions difficult.

Accessibility solution: Use simple language, readable fonts, clear headings, and consistent page layouts.

👂

04 · Hearing Impairment

Deaf or hard-of-hearing users may not hear spoken information, sound effects, or audio instructions.

Accessibility solution: Provide captions, transcripts, and visual alternatives for important sound information.

ACCESSIBILITY BENEFITS EVERYONE

Why Accessibility Matters

Better for everyone

Captions help people watching videos in noisy places. Clear contrast makes text easier to read in bright sunlight. Keyboard support can help users who prefer not to use a mouse.

Inclusive web design

Accessibility removes unnecessary barriers. It helps more people access information, education, communication, and online services independently.

KEY POINTS

What We Learned

  • Accessibility supports people with visual, motor, cognitive, and hearing disabilities.
  • Screen readers and keyboard navigation help users access websites in different ways.
  • Captions, readable text, good contrast, and meaningful alt text improve usability.
  • Accessible design benefits people with and without disabilities.
